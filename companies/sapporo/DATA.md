# Sapporo Holdings (2501) - Public Data
Gathered Oct 17, 2026. ¥ billion unless noted. Dec fiscal year. **IFRS.** 5-for-1 stock split on Jan 1, 2026.
Source quality: most 2025-26 numbers come from Quartr's AI-written summaries; FY2024 from the company's results PDF. **Verify in the English IR pages before using.**

## Standing in 8 bullets
- Japanese beer and drinks company (Sapporo, Yebisu), also food and soft drinks, restaurants, and until now a large real estate business.
- It is selling most of the real estate business. Real estate is now reported as "discontinued operations", which means it is removed from sales and operating profit.
- Net income is distorted: H1 FY26 net income was 295.4 because of a one-time gain from losing control of real estate. Underlying profit is much smaller.
- Real core profit: core operating profit (operating profit before one-off items) H1 +37% to 6.8. Full-year guide 22.0 (down 12% from 25.0).
- Operating profit is guided at only 6.0, because of one-off restructuring costs (mostly US) and impairments.
- Growth bright spots: domestic beer volume +5% in H1; Sapporo-brand overseas volume +15% (North America beer +13%, Asia +20%).
- Plans: a Carlsberg partnership, a new medium-term plan for 2027-2030, and use of the proceeds (300-400 billion yen is "earmarked" for drinks growth, debt reduction, and shareholder returns).
- Hardest of your three to forecast net income for, because the one-time items decide it.

### 1.4 Real estate sale and deals
- Majority of Sapporo Real Estate sold in stages (2026-2029) to Spark LLC (backed by PAG and KKR), valued at about ¥477B (Drinks Intel). Sapporo keeps 30% of Yebisu Garden Place.
- Expected one-time gain about ¥330B (Drinks Intel). First closing expected June 1 (Quartr).
- Carlsberg: partnership and joint venture; one summary says a USD 643M investment for a 25% stake (what the stake is in is unclear - verify).
- US: restructuring, transfer of the Stone brand, impairments.

## 2. Things that can move it (outside)
- **Weather:** a hot summer lifts beer sales.
- **Industry demand:** beer-type shipments (Japan Brewers Association publishes them), long-run decline in beer, shift to RTD (ready-to-drink) and non-alcohol.
- **Tax and price changes:** the Oct 2026 beer-type tax unification (from my own knowledge, not checked in a source) and price revisions; demand pulled forward before price rises.
- **Costs:** barley, aluminum, packaging, energy, logistics.
- **FX:** weaker yen helps overseas profit and raises import costs.
- **Overseas:** US beer market, Asia growth.
- **Capital deployment:** what it buys with the proceeds.
